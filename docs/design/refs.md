What the heat sim taught against these (2026-11-09): simulated heat alone gives an established channel one even orange core, which no frame shows; the Kilauea frame's netted grey skin and the Mauna Loa frame's open red with hot spots both come from a skin carried rigidly on the core, closing as the sim cools, while the sim keeps the banks crusted and the breach and pool the brightest.

Heat colours (USGS): yellow about 1000-1200 C, orange 800-1000, red 600-800, black when cooled.

Night sky from the earlier footage notes: blue-violet, not green-black; the plume is orange-lit from beneath; the
fountain core is yellow-white; channels go yellow-white at the vent, orange, then red downstream; the glow is a thin
ribbon on mostly black ground.

What the network pass must take from the two 2026-11-09 frames: confluences at shallow angles with the ground between feeders closing to a point, widths that add below a join without doubling, every strand ending in a join or the pool; and a pool that is crusted grey netted with orange seams, hot only in a fan where the trunk enters, dark at the far shore.

What the network pass took from those two frames, as built (2026-11-09; the spec's section 13 has every constant, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-lava-network-design.md`):

- From the Waimakariri river: a tributary meets the trunk at a shallow angle (aimed at 18 to 26 degrees, tested against 15 to 28, never over 30) and turns onto the trunk's heading over its last stretch, so the ground between the two banks closes to a rounded tip (the fillet fan) and not to a notch. The widths add as rivers add and do not double: `(wt^1.5 + wf^1.5)^(1/1.5)`, with a 1.1 bulge on the outer bank below the join, so a desktop trunk is 1.66 W0 before the pool's flare and 2.0 W0 at the pool. Every stream ends in a join or in the pool; where the geometry cannot bring a feeder in under 30 degrees it runs to the pool on its own (a 276 px cone joins its rim stream 46 percent of the time and its mid stream 42). The banks run together for a median of about 11 feeder widths before a join; a 3 width contact was out of reach under the arrival band and the 12 degree chord rule together.
- From the Halemaumau lake: the pool is crusted and netted with seams, hot only in a fan where a stream enters (the sim's entry fan) and dark at the far shore (the pool cools twice as fast along its radius, so the far half crusts). The rock above its arc is lit by it (a bloom, clipped so it never lights the pool itself), and its far half is cut off by the canvas's bottom edge on a desktop (the silhouette, foreground ridge included, is painted under the lava, so no ridge hides it) and by the screen's bottom edge on a phone. The crust drifts outward at a steady rate; that rate (1.2 x the flow's speed) is predicted from a model and is not yet confirmed live.
